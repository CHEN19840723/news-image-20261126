### Lead — US fires new tariff salvo at 14 trading partners (Wednesday)
- President Trump on Wednesday proposed new Section 301 duties on 14
  major trading partners — China, Japan, the EU and others — to fight
  what the US calls excess factory capacity. China would face the
  highest capped rate, 20% on top of roughly 25% from Trump's first
  term, rising to 40% if the US-China trade truce (which expires
  10 January) unravels. The EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and
  Switzerland would face 15%; Vietnam 20% if a deal is reached, 35%
  if talks fail; India 17% with a deal, 25% without. The duties would
  not take effect before a public hearing on 25 January 2027. The
  move rebuilds Trump's tariff leverage under Section 301 after the
  Supreme Court struck down his broad emergency-powers duties in
  February.

### Technology — Microsoft declares "Code Red" after outage (reported Tuesday)
- Microsoft declared a company-wide "Code Red" after a 24-hour
  global outage on 12–13 November disrupted Exchange Online,
  Outlook, Teams and Copilot for governments, trading firms and
  airlines — the worst email outage in the service's history,
  according to internal memos reviewed by Reuters and reported on
  Tuesday. The cause was an undetected Windows scalability defect
  introduced in July, not a cyberattack. Microsoft put reliability
  ahead of new features, with five workstreams and a target of
  worldwide recovery within one hour for future incidents.

### Strip — All hostages freed in Cartagena siege (Wednesday)
- Colombian special forces stormed an Éxito supermarket in Cartagena
  early on Wednesday, ending a 17-hour siege and freeing the last 20
  hostages; one gunman was killed and four detained. The siege began
  on Tuesday morning when gunmen, after failing to rob an armoured
  car, took about 30 people hostage and demanded COP 7 billion
  (about $2 million). About ten hostages had been released on Tuesday.
